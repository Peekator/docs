A Click Test question shows respondents an image and asks them to click on specific areas. The results are aggregated into a heatmap showing where respondents clicked most often. This is a core UX research method for testing navigation, visual hierarchy, and the findability of elements.

## When to use this question type

- You want to see where respondents naturally click on a design, screenshot, or mock-up.
- You need to test whether important buttons, links, or messages are found quickly.
- You want visual evidence of confusion or misclicks to guide design improvements.

## How to add it

<Steps>
  <Step>
    In the questionnaire builder, click **Add questions**.
  </Step>
  <Step>
    Select **Click Test** from the popup.
  </Step>
  <Step>
    The question appears on the canvas. Click it to open the configuration panel and upload the image and set the task instruction.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Settings

| Setting | Description |
| --- | --- |
| Image | The image, screenshot, or design respondents will click on. |
| Task instruction | The prompt telling respondents what to do, for example, "Where would you click to buy this product?" |
| Allow multiple clicks | Lets respondents click more than once on the image. |
| Click limit | The maximum number of clicks allowed if multiple clicks are enabled. |
| Show heatmap to respondent | Displays a heatmap overlay after the respondent clicks. |

<Note>
  **Needs product verification:** Confirm whether "Define correct zones" and "Time limit per click" settings exist for this question type.
</Note>

## What respondents see

Respondents see an image with a task instruction above or below it. They click on the image where they think the answer lies. If multiple clicks are allowed, they can click several times. A counter may show remaining clicks if a limit is set. After clicking, they proceed to the next question.

<Warning>
  Click Test questions involve image rendering and coordinate capture. Always preview and test the full questionnaire before launching to ensure clicks register correctly across devices and screen sizes.
</Warning>

## Logic and piping

You can use the click coordinates or whether a click fell within a defined zone in logic to show or hide later questions. For example, if a respondent clicks outside the correct zone, you can ask a follow-up about what they were looking for. For full instructions, see [Logic and branching](/questionnaire/logic-and-branching).

## Results in reporting

Results appear as a heatmap overlay on the image, with darker or warmer colours indicating areas of high click density. Individual click coordinates are also available in exports. If correct zones were defined, accuracy rates are shown.

## Example use case

An e-commerce retailer tests a new product page. Respondents are asked, "Where would you click to add this item to your basket?" The heatmap reveals that many respondents click on the product image instead of the "Add to basket" button, indicating a need for clearer visual hierarchy.
