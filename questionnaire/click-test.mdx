## When to use this question type

Use a Click test when you want to:

- Test attention on an advert, package, design or image.
- Check whether respondents can find a button, product or message.
- Compare which areas of an image attract clicks.
- Measure how quickly respondents find the intended area.

## Add a Click test question

1. In the questionnaire builder, select **Add questions**.
2. Select **Click test**.
3. Enter your question text.
4. Edit the **Instruction note** if needed.
5. Upload the image respondents will click on.
6. Select **Save question**.

<Info>
  You can use one image per Click test. Uploading a new image removes the previous one.
</Info>

## Mark a success area

<Card title="About the success area" type="info">
  A **success area** is the part of the image you want respondents to find or select. It gives you a clear benchmark: in reporting, clicks inside this area count as successful, while clicks outside it do not.

  Use it to test whether a button, product, message or design element is easy to notice. Leave it unset if you only want to explore where attention goes naturally.
</Card>

Select **Mark success area** below the image to define the part of the image respondents should find or select.

Drag and resize the highlighted area to cover the relevant element. You can remove it at any time by selecting **Remove success area**.

Use a success area when you need to measure how many respondents clicked the intended part of the image.

## Conditions

Use **Add conditional rule** under **Ask only if** to control whether the Click test is shown, based on an earlier answer.

For more information, see [Logic and branching](/questionnaire/logic-and-branching).

## What respondents see

Respondents see the image and select the area or areas that answer the question. They must make the number of clicks you set in the settings.

## Results in reporting

Results show the tested image with the success area, where one has been set. You can see:

- The percentage of respondents who clicked within the success area.
- The median time respondents took to click.
- The overall pattern of clicks across the image.

## Example use case

A detergent brand shows a shelf image and asks, “Imagine you are shopping for laundry detergent. Click on the packaging part that first catches your attention.”

The brand marks its product label as the success area to see how many respondents noticed it first and how quickly they found it.
