## When to use this question type

Use a Card sort question when you want to:

- Understand how respondents group products, features or ideas.
- Test whether your categories make sense to respondents.
- Prioritise benefits or attributes.
- Identify which items respondents see as similar.

## Add a Card sort question

1. In the questionnaire builder, select **Add questions**.
2. Select **Card sort**.
3. Enter your question text.
4. Edit the **Instruction note** if needed.
5. Choose the card sort type.
6. Add your categories and cards.
7. Select **Save question**.

## Add categories and cards

Categories are the groups respondents sort cards into. For example, a detergent survey might use **Essential**, **Nice to have** and **Not important to me**.

Cards are the items respondents place into those categories, such as “Removes tough stains” or “Works well at low temperatures”.

To add an associated card, select **Add card to this category** beneath the relevant category.

Use associated cards when you have a predefined categorisation that you want to test. This allows Peekator to report agreement between your expected placement and respondents’ placements.

Add standalone cards in the **Cards** section when you want respondents to decide where they belong without a predefined answer.

Select **Add category** to create another category. You can reorder or remove categories and cards at any time.

## What respondents see

Respondents drag or select each card and place it into the category that best matches their view. In a Hybrid category sort, they can also create their own categories.

## Results in reporting

Card sort results include three views:

  </tbody>
</table>

## Example use case

A detergent brand asks respondents to sort benefits into **Essential**, **Nice to have** and **Not important to me**. Cards include stain removal, low temperature performance, recyclable packaging and fragrance.

The results show which benefits matter most, which are commonly grouped together and whether respondents agree with the brand’s expected categorisation.
