A Quadrant Matrix question presents a two-by-two grid with labelled axes and asks respondents to drag items into the quadrant that best represents their view. This is a visual and intuitive way to map perceptions, priorities, or positioning, such as importance versus satisfaction or price versus quality.

## When to use this question type

- You want to visualise how respondents perceive trade-offs between two dimensions.
- You need a perceptual map of brands, features, or statements from the respondent's point of view.
- You want an engaging, interactive question that produces immediately chartable data.

## How to add it

<Steps>
  <Step>
    In the questionnaire builder, click **Add questions**.
  </Step>
  <Step>
    Select **Quadrant Matrix** from the popup.
  </Step>
  <Step>
    The question appears on the canvas. Click it to open the configuration panel and define the axes, labels, and items to place.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Settings

| Setting | Description |
| --- | --- |
| Question text | The label respondents see. |
| Horizontal axis label | The label for the left-right dimension, for example, "Low price" to "High price". |
| Vertical axis label | The label for the bottom-top dimension, for example, "Low quality" to "High quality". |
| Items to place | The list of items respondents drag onto the grid, for example, brand names or features. |
| Mandatory | Requires all items to be placed before the respondent can continue. |
| Randomise items | Shuffles the order of items for each respondent. |
| Allow multiple items per quadrant | Lets respondents place more than one item in the same quadrant. |

<Note>
  **Needs product verification:** Confirm whether "Snap to grid" and "Show axis midpoints" settings exist for this question type.
</Note>

## What respondents see

Respondents see a square grid with axis labels on the edges and a list of draggable items beside it. They drag each item into one of the four quadrants. If mandatory, they must place all items before continuing. The interface may show a counter of remaining items.

<Warning>
  Quadrant Matrix questions involve drag-and-drop interaction and axis validation. Always preview and test the full questionnaire before launching to ensure items, axes, and placement rules behave correctly across devices.
</Warning>

## Logic and piping

You can use the quadrant placement of specific items in logic to show or hide later questions. For example, if a respondent places your brand in the "High price, Low quality" quadrant, you can branch to a diagnostic question. For full instructions, see [Logic and branching](/questionnaire/logic-and-branching).

## Results in reporting

Results appear as an aggregate perceptual map showing the percentage of respondents who placed each item in each quadrant. Individual placements are available in exports. This makes it easy to identify consensus positions and outlier perceptions.

## Example use case

A consumer goods company asks respondents to place five competing brands on a quadrant with "Low price" to "High price" on the horizontal axis and "Low quality" to "High quality" on the vertical axis. The results reveal that most respondents place the company's brand in the "High price, High quality" quadrant, supporting a premium positioning strategy.
