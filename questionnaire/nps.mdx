A Net Promoter Score (NPS) question measures how likely respondents are to recommend a product, service, or brand to others. Respondents rate on a scale from 0 to 10. The results classify respondents as Promoters (9-10), Passives (7-8), or Detractors (0-6), producing a single loyalty score.

## When to use this question type

- You want a standardised benchmark for customer loyalty or satisfaction.
- You need a metric that is easy to track over time and compare across segments.
- You want to identify Detractors who may need follow-up attention.

## How to add it

<Steps>
  <Step>
    In the questionnaire builder, click **Add questions**.
  </Step>
  <Step>
    Select **NPS** from the popup.
  </Step>
  <Step>
    The question appears on the canvas. Click it to open the configuration panel and edit the question text and labels.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Settings

| Setting | Description |
| --- | --- |
| Question text | The main question, typically "How likely are you to recommend [brand] to a friend or colleague?" |
| Low label | The label for 0, for example, "Not at all likely". |
| High label | The label for 10, for example, "Extremely likely". |
| Mandatory | Requires a selection before the respondent can continue. |
| Follow-up open end | Adds a text box asking why the respondent gave that score. |

<Note>
  **Needs product verification:** Confirm whether "Scale direction" (0 on left vs right) and "Custom scale range" settings exist for this question type.
</Note>

## What respondents see

Respondents see a horizontal scale numbered 0 to 10 with the low and high labels at each end. They click one number. If a follow-up open end is enabled, a text box appears after they select a score, asking for the reason behind their rating.

## Logic and piping

You can use the NPS score in logic to show or hide later questions. For example, you can branch Detractors (0-6) to a service recovery question and Promoters (9-10) to a referral request. For full instructions, see [Logic and branching](/questionnaire/logic-and-branching).

## Results in reporting

Results show the overall NPS score (percentage of Promoters minus percentage of Detractors), a distribution bar chart, and the count in each segment. If a follow-up question was included, responses are listed for qualitative analysis.

## Example use case

A SaaS company asks, "How likely are you to recommend our platform to a colleague?" Detractors are routed to a follow-up asking what would improve their experience. The company tracks the NPS monthly and benchmarks it against industry averages.
