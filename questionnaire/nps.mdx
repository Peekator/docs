## When to use an NPS question

- You want to measure loyalty or likelihood to recommend.
- You want a recognised benchmark for customer sentiment.
- You need to track NPS over time.

## Add an NPS question

1. In the Questionnaire builder, select **Add question**.
2. Select **Net Promoter Score (NPS)**.
3. Enter your question text.
4. Add an optional **Instruction note** if respondents need extra guidance.
5. Add labels for the two ends of the scale:
   - **Min label** explains what a score of 0 means.
   - **Max label** explains what a score of 10 means.

For example:

- Min label: Not at all likely
- Max label: Extremely likely

<Warning>
  NPS uses a fixed 0 to 10 scale. You cannot change the answer options.
</Warning>

## Add conditions

Use **Add conditional rule** in the **Ask only if** section to control whether respondents see the NPS question.

For full instructions, see [Logic and branching](https://chatgpt.com/c/6a9944be-99a0-83ed-acda-754df5c50a8c#).

## What respondents see

Respondents select one score from 0 to 10.

The labels you add help them understand what the lowest and highest scores represent.

## Results in reporting

Peekator automatically calculates an NPS score from -100 to 100.

Responses are grouped into:

| Group | Scores |
| :-- | :-- |
| Ambassadors | 9 to 10 |
| Neutrals | 7 to 8 |
| Haters | 0 to 6 |

Your NPS score is calculated by subtracting the percentage of **Haters** from the percentage of **Ambassadors**.

Reporting shows:

- Your overall NPS score.
- The percentage of Ambassadors, Neutrals and Haters.
- A visual NPS gauge.
- The distribution of responses from 0 to 10.

Select **All answers view** to review each score in more detail. You can switch between percentages and response counts.

## Example

Ask: “On a scale of 0 to 10, how likely are you to recommend Peekator to a colleague?”

- Min label: Not at all likely
- Max label: Extremely likely
