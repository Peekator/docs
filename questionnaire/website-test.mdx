## When to use this question type

Use a Website Test when you want to:

- Observe how respondents explore a website.
- Test whether they can find information or complete a task.
- Understand what attracts attention and why respondents make particular choices.
- Collect spoken feedback while respondents browse.

## Add a Website Test question

1. In the questionnaire builder, select **Add questions**.
2. Select **Website Test**.
3. Enter the task you want respondents to complete.
4. Edit the **Instruction note** if needed.
5. Enter the **Web page URL** respondents will visit.
6. Select **Save question**.

## Write a clear task

Give respondents a realistic goal instead of telling them exactly where to click.

For example:

> Imagine you are choosing a cleaning product for your household. Explore the website to find a product you would consider buying, then look for information on how much to use in a wash.

Use the instruction note to ask respondents to think aloud, for example:

> Please speak your thoughts aloud as you browse. Tell us what you are looking at, what you are trying to find and why you choose to click somewhere.

## Configure audio and screen sharing

Select **Require audio feedback and screen-share where supported** to require spoken feedback while the respondent browses the website.

Respondents speak as they scroll through the website. Where their device supports it, Peekator also captures their screen share.

## What respondents see

Respondents receive your task and open the website you have provided. They browse the website, complete the task and describe their thoughts as they go.

## Results in reporting

Results include an AI generated summary for each respondent, based on their voice recording. The summary helps you review what respondents did, what they noticed and why they made particular choices.

Select the response icon beside a summary to view the full answer. Select **Website Link** to open the tested website.

## Example use case

A retailer asks respondents to find a product they would consider buying, then locate its usage information. The spoken feedback shows whether the product range is easy to browse, which details influence decisions and whether the usage information is easy to find.
