## When to use MaxDiff

Use a MaxDiff question when:

- You need respondents to make trade-offs between items.
- You want to understand relative priorities, rather than ratings for every item.
- You have many items and want a more focused alternative to asking respondents to rank the full list.

## Add a MaxDiff question

1. In the questionnaire builder, select **Add questions**.
2. Choose **MaxDiff**.
3. Enter the question text.
4. Add the items respondents will compare.

<Tip>
  You can paste all items into the first answer field. Peekator separates them into individual answers automatically.
</Tip>

## Configure the question

Set the labels respondents will use for their choices:

- **Text for worst answer** — the label for the least valuable or least important item, such as _Worst_ or _Least important_.
- **Text for best answer** — the label for the most valuable or most important item, such as _Best_ or _Most important_.

## Conditional logic

Under **Ask only if**, select **Add conditional rule** to control whether the entire MaxDiff question is shown, based on an earlier answer.

For more information, see [Logic and branching](/questionnaire/logic-and-branching).

## What respondents see

Respondents see several sets of items. For each set, they select:

- one item as the most valuable or important; and
- one item as the least valuable or important.

Items can appear more than once across the sets. This is intentional: repeated comparisons produce a more reliable view of relative priorities.

## Results in reporting

Results show each item with:

- the percentage selected as **LEAST important**;
- the percentage selected as **MOST important**; and
- a **Utility Score**.

The Utility Score compares the item’s overall likelihood of being chosen as most rather than least important. Higher scores indicate stronger relative importance; lower scores indicate weaker relative importance.

Use the table to identify the clearest priorities, items with mixed views, and items that are consistently selected as least important.

## Example use case

A product team wants to understand which service improvements customers value most. They add items such as faster delivery, lower prices, more support channels and easier returns.

With MaxDiff, customers choose the most and least valuable improvement from several small sets. The results give the team a clear relative priority order for future investment.
