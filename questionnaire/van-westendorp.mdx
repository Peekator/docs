## When to use Van Westendorp

Use this question when you need to explore pricing for a product, service, subscription or ticket.

It helps you understand:

- the price range respondents consider acceptable;
- the point at which the offer feels like a bargain;
- when the price starts to feel expensive; and
- when it becomes too expensive to consider.

## Add a Van Westendorp question

1. In the questionnaire builder, select **Add questions**.
2. Choose **Van Westendorp**.
3. Enter the question text.
4. Review and edit the four price questions if needed.
5. Set the price range and currency.

An **Instruction note** is added automatically. You can edit it to give respondents additional guidance.

## Conditional logic

Under **Ask only if**, select **Add conditional rule** to control whether the entire Van Westendorp question is shown, based on an earlier answer.

For more information, see [Logic and branching](/questionnaire/logic-and-branching).

## What respondents see

Respondents use sliders to select a price for each of the four prompts.

They consider the same product or service throughout, moving from the point at which it feels too cheap to the point at which it feels too expensive.

## Results in reporting

Results show four price-sensitivity curves, one for each price prompt.

Peekator also calculates:

- **Acceptable price range** — the lower and upper prices respondents are generally willing to accept;
- **Optimum price point** — the price that best balances concerns that the offer is too cheap or too expensive; and
- **Indifference price point** — the point at which similar shares of respondents see the price as expensive and as a bargain.

Use these results as an evidence-based starting point for pricing decisions alongside costs, positioning and commercial objectives.

## Example use case

A fitness app is considering the monthly price for a premium subscription. It asks potential customers when the price would feel too cheap, like good value, expensive but acceptable, and too expensive.

The results show an acceptable price range and help the team choose a subscription price that balances perceived value with demand.
