Gabor Granger tests willingness to pay by presenting a fixed question with different price points to respondents, each answering Yes or No. Aggregating responses across respondents produces a demand curve showing what percentage would pay at each price level.

Use Gabor Granger when you need quantifiable pricing data for a product with a defined feature set.

## Add a Gabor Granger question

<Steps>
  <Step title="Click Add Question">
    In the questionnaire builder, click **Add Question** and select **Gabor Granger**.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Review the default question text">
    Peekator pre-fills: `Would you pay this much for this product / service?` Edit this to reference your specific product.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Add price points">
    Add each price point as a sub-question. Each sub-question represents one price level shown to respondents.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Settings

| Setting | Description |
| --- | --- |
| **Price points** | The price levels to test. Add one sub-question per price point (for example: €5, €10, €15, €20). |
| **Answers** | Fixed: **Yes** (willing to pay) and **No** (not willing to pay). These cannot be changed. |

<Note>
  The default instruction shown to respondents is: "Select Yes or No."
</Note>

## How results work

Peekator calculates the percentage of respondents who answered Yes at each price point. As price increases, the percentage willing to pay decreases. Multiplying the Yes percentage by the price at each point gives an estimated revenue index, helping identify the revenue-maximizing price.
