## When to use Gabor Granger

Use this question when you want to test pricing for a product, service or subscription.

It helps you understand:

- willingness to pay at each proposed price;
- how demand changes as the price increases; and
- the price point with the greatest estimated revenue.

## Add a Gabor Granger question

1. In the questionnaire builder, select **Add questions**.
2. Choose **Gabor Granger**.
3. Enter the question text.
4. Add the price points you want to test under **Values**.
5. Review the positive and negative answers under **Answers**.

<Info>
  An **Instruction note** is added automatically. You can edit it to give respondents additional guidance.
</Info>

## Configure price points and answers

Under **Values**, add each proposed price that you want respondents to consider.

Use a sensible range of prices and keep the increments meaningful. For example: £10, £15, £20, £25 and £30.

Under **Answers**, define the response options:

## Conditional logic

Under **Ask only if**, select **Add conditional rule** to control whether the entire Gabor Granger question is shown, based on an earlier answer.

For more information, see [Logic and branching](/questionnaire/logic-and-branching).

## What respondents see

Respondents are shown a proposed price and select the answer that best reflects whether they would be willing to pay it.

They see different price points during the question, allowing Peekator to measure demand at each level.

## Results in reporting

Results appear in **Fundamental Analytics** as a chart showing:

- **Willingness to Pay** — the share of respondents willing to pay each price;
- **Revenue** — the estimated revenue at each price point; and
- **Optimum price point** — the price with the highest estimated revenue.

The revenue line is calculated from the revenue base shown above the chart. Hover over a point to see the value for that price.

## Example use case

A software company is considering monthly prices of £15, £20, £25, £30 and £35 for a new plan.

It asks respondents whether they would pay each price. The results show how willingness to pay changes across the range and identify the price with the highest estimated revenue.
